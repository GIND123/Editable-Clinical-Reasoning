\begin{abstract}
Language models increasingly explain their clinical reasoning, but the explanation is often not what produces the answer, and models frequently defer to users against the evidence. We introduce \emph{causal editability}: a clinical model's diagnosis should follow valid corrections to its reasoning, flag corrections that contradict the record, and ignore irrelevant ones, and it should do so because the decision is computed through that reasoning. We build ClinBlueprint, a 4--8-billion-parameter model whose diagnosis is mediated by a typed, clinician-editable reasoning graph, and ClinEdit-Faith, a benchmark of edits with physician-derived expected effects from two hospitals. ClinBlueprint matches same-size chain-of-thought models in accuracy \res{X}, follows valid edits \res{X\%} vs \res{Y\%}, flags \res{X\%} of contradicting assertions at a \res{Y\%} false-flag rate, and routes \res{X\%} of diagnostic information through the graph. In a randomized crossover study, clinicians detected \res{X} more model errors with the graph than with text explanations.
\end{abstract}
